# Scoring model

Two axes, reported separately, never averaged.

## Tie strength: will they say yes?

Signals available in a LinkedIn export are thin. Ranked by what they
actually predict:

| Signal | Weight | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Tie age 4y+ | 45 | Survived job changes. Predates the content era. Almost always a real interaction. |
| Tie age 2-4y | 32 | Likely a real interaction, context may have faded. |
| Tie age 1-2y | 18 | Could go either way. |
| Tie age 3-12mo | 8 | Low context. |
| Tie under 3mo | 3 | Most likely an inbound follow off a post. Not a relationship. |
| Email exported | 12 | They opted into sharing it. Weak but real openness signal. |
| Founder or C-level | 8 | Can decide to make the intro without checking with anyone. |

Tie age is the primary axis, not a tiebreaker. For anyone who posts
regularly, recent connections are audience, not network, and scoring
them as warm ties is the fastest way to produce a list that gets
ignored after the first three failed asks.

## Path relevance: is the intro useful?

Target mode only. Seniority times 9, plus a scarcity adjustment:
being the only route into an account raises a path's value, and being
junior at a company already reached higher up lowers it.

## Reverse mode scores differently, on purpose

Target mode rewards scarcity, because one route in is still a route.
Reverse mode must not: scarcity plus seniority is a perfect description
of a solo founder of a one-person company, which ranks at the top of
both axes while being worth nothing commercially.

Reverse mode therefore gates on plurality (2+ contacts) and ranks on
best tie strength plus depth, where depth counts contacts, senior
contacts, and ties older than two years. Whether the account is worth
pursuing at all is not knowable from the file and is left to the
judgment pass.
